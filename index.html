About us

Why we made this site

Math for IT puts five math topics in one place for IT students. Each topic lists its main ideas, states what you should be able to do afterward, and gives you a small tool to practice with instead of only reading.

Our goal is simple: to show that math is a working tool in computing, from networks and data to security and AI.

Topic 1 of 5

Graphs and Networks

  • Graph theory basics (vertices, edges, paths)A graph is dots, called vertices, joined by lines, called edges. A path is a walk along the edges.
  • Euler and Hamiltonian circuitsAn Euler circuit uses every edge once. A Hamiltonian circuit visits every vertex once.
  • Network applications (computer and data networks)Computers and routers are the vertices. Cables and links are the edges.

Learning outcome

Analyze and represent networks using graph theory.

Try it: Euler paths

Tap a line to remove it or add it back. The degree of a vertex is how many edges touch it.

Statistics and Data Analysis

  • Data organization, tables, chartsPut raw numbers into tables and charts so patterns are easy to see.
  • Measures of central tendency and dispersionMean, median and mode show the center. Range and standard deviation show how spread out the data is.
  • Probability and risk analysisHow likely something is to happen, and how costly it is if it does.

Learning outcome

Apply statistical tools in data analytics and computing projects.

Topic 4 of 5

Geometry, Measurement, and Spatial Technology

  • Euclidean and non-Euclidean geometryEuclidean geometry follows the rules of a flat plane. Non-Euclidean geometry uses other rules, such as moving only along city blocks or on a curved surface.
  • Application in graphics, animation, and GPSShapes, movement and location on a screen or a map are all calculated with geometry.

Learning outcome

Apply geometric principles in computer graphics and spatial analysis.

Try it: two ways to measure distance

Point A is fixed at (1, 1). Move point B and compare the straight line with the city-block path.

Straight line
City-block path

Solid line: Euclidean distance, the shortest way on a flat plane. Dashed path: taxicab distance, moving only along the grid. GPS also measures distance on a curved Earth, where flat-plane rules are only an approximation.

Topic 5 of 5

Modern Applications of Mathematics

  • Cryptography and cybersecurityMath that scrambles data so only the right person can read it.
  • Algorithms and artificial intelligenceStep-by-step rules that computers follow, and the math that lets AI learn from data.
  • Mathematical modeling in environment and healthEquations that describe how things like weather or the spread of a disease change over time.

Learning outcome

Demonstrate the application of mathematics in cybersecurity and AI.

Try it: Caesar cipher

A key moves every letter forward in the alphabet. Change the key and the message.

Encrypted message

Letter map (shaded letters replace the ones above them)

This cipher is easy to break. Modern security uses much harder math, such as very large prime numbers, but the idea is the same: a key turns readable text into scrambled text.
